When I was six, festivals in our village had a different kind of magic. Children would sit on their grandfathers' bicycles, holding their waists tightly as they rode through crowded streets filled with lights, music, and laughter. I used to stand near our gate with my younger brother beside me, watching them pass by one after another.Some children returned with spinning tops, balloons, wooden toys, and sugarcandies in their hands. Their faces glowed with excitement as they talked about the giant wheel rides and crowded stalls. My brother and I never cried. Not even once. Because our father never let us feel deprived. Whenever he took us to festivals, he bought us everything we pointed at without hesitation. We probably had more toys than many of those children. Yet... Somewhere deep inside, there was a silence we never understood at that age. A small untold pain. Why was everyone going with their grandparents except us? Back then, I didn't know enough about relationships, society, or family wounds. I was too young to understand why certain distances existed between people. So instead of questioning it, we simply stayedquiet and watched the roads empty after the crowd disappeared. Years passed. We grew up. Years later, One random day, during a family argument related to grandparents and affection, my mother suddenly broke down. I still remember her voice trembling. "My children never experienced a grandfather's love," she said softly. "But not once did they ask me why. Not once did they question their father." 3 Her eyes were filled with tears she had probably hidden for years. "And do you know how painful that is for a mother? Watching your children silently miss something they deserved?" The room fell silent. "I raised my children with kindness and respect," she continued. "Today they know how to love people genuinely. They don't need anyone else's affection to become good human beings." That moment broke something inside me. Suddenly, all those festival memories came rushing back - the bicycles, the laughter, the silent watching. And I realized... And for the first time in my life, I realized my mother had noticed every single moment we thought she hadn't. She carried that guilt quietly all these years while making sure we never felt unloved. My throat tightened so badly that I couldn't even speak. But my tears spoke for me. Even today, I'm in my last teenage years, yet my mother still feeds me breakfast, lunch, and dinner with her own hands whenever she can. People often scold her. "You're not teaching her household responsibilities." "She's old enough to do things herself." But my mother simply smiles and says, "Maybe she's grown up for the world... but as long as I'm beside her, she needs nothing. For me, she'll always be my three-year-old baby." And honestly? Those words hit harder than anything else. Because behind that softness is a mother who spent her entire life protecting her children from pains they never even noticed.
